# Pharmacovigilance Case Study & Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)

**Project Milestone Submission:** ADR Identification, ICSR, Causality Assessment & Severity Classification  
**Author:** Arjun Chakrabortty , student of pharmacy

---

## 1. Case Scenario Overview
* **Patient Demographics:** 58-year-old male
* **Primary Suspect Drug:** Allopurinol 300 mg once daily (Started 5 days prior for asymptomatic hyperuricemia)
* **Concomitant Medications:** Amlodipine 5 mg once daily (stable for 2 years for essential hypertension)
* **Adverse Event (AE):** Generalized maculopapular rash, facial angioedema, fever (38.5°C), and elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST 3x upper limit of normal).
* **Clinical Outcome:** Allopurinol was immediately discontinued. The patient was hospitalized, treated with systemic corticosteroids and antihistamines, and achieved complete resolution of symptoms within 14 days.

---

## 2. ADR Identification & Case Classification
* **Adverse Drug Reaction:** Allopurinol-induced Hypersensitivity Syndrome / Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS).
* **Severity Classification:** **Serious**
  * *Justification:* The event required inpatient hospitalization, resulted in prolongation of hospital stay, and was a medically significant systemic reaction.

---

## 3. Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) Narrative
> A 58-year-old male experienced a serious adverse event characterized by a generalized maculopapular rash, facial angioedema, pyrexia (38.5°C), and elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST 3x ULN), occurring 5 days after initiating Allopurinol 300 mg daily for hyperuricemia. The patient was concomitantly receiving Amlodipine 5 mg daily. Upon recognition of the reaction, Allopurinol was withdrawn. The patient required hospital admission and intravenous/systemic therapy, leading to full recovery 14 days post-cessation. 

---

## 4. Causality Assessment (WHO-UMC & Naranjo Scale)
* **Assessment Result:** **Probable / Likely**
* **Clinical Rationale:**
  * **Time to Onset:** 5 days post-initiation of Allopurinol, matching the classic presentation window for severe cutaneous adverse reactions.
  * **Dechallenge:** Positive response; symptoms resolved rapidly following the withdrawal of Allopurinol.
  * **Alternative Causes:** Concomitant amlodipine has a long-term stable history and was continued without re-challenge issues; viral etiologies and other confounders were evaluated and ruled out.
  * **Known Drug Profile:** Allopurinol is well-documented in pharmacovigilance literature for inducing severe hypersensitivity reactions.

---

## 5. Risk Management Plan (RMP) & Minimization
* **Routine Risk Minimization:** Ensure updated Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) and Patient Information Leaflets (PIL) explicitly highlight early warning signs of severe skin reactions.
* **Additional Risk Minimization:** Implement pre-treatment screening for the *HLA-B*58:01* allele in high-risk patient populations prior to initiating allopurinol therapy to prevent severe hypersensitivity reactions.
